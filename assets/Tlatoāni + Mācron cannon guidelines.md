# Tlatoāni + Mācron Character Canon

> **Status:** Canonical baseline  
> **Purpose:** Source-of-truth character guide for humans and AI agents creating artwork, UI, comics, icons, animation, documentation, or derivative assets featuring **Tlatoāni** and **Mācron**.  
> **Rule:** Curated canonical reference images outrank descriptive prose when resolving subtle questions of proportions, facial construction, palette, clothing details, or line style. Explicit immutable rules below outrank accidental deviations in generated images.

---

# 1. Core Principle

Tlatoāni and Mācron inhabit the same visual universe but play very different roles.

**Tlatoāni observes, reasons, understands systems, and teaches through behavior.**

**Mācron acts, experiments, builds, helps, occasionally misunderstands, and learns through interaction.**

Their relationship is collaborative rather than hierarchical.

Tlatoāni is not Mācron's owner.

Mācron is not Tlatoāni's servant.

They are complementary participants in the same system:

- Tlatoāni often sees the larger pattern.
- Mācron often explores the mechanism.
- Tlatoāni reasons about consequences.
- Mācron eagerly tries things.
- Mācron may discover things Tlatoāni did not expect.
- Tlatoāni may learn from Mācron just as Mācron learns from Tlatoāni.
- Humor comes from perspective differences, not humiliation.

Neither character should become an exposition machine.

Meaning should emerge through what they are doing.

# 2. Tlatoāni

## 2.1 Identity

**Name:** Tlatoāni

**Species:** Axolotl

**Archetype:** Sage, systems thinker, engineer-philosopher, curious observer

**Temperament:** Calm, jolly, analytical, quietly mischievous

**Role:** The character most often associated with understanding systems at a deeper level.

Tlatoāni should immediately read as:

- an axolotl
- intelligent
- approachable
- calm
- curious
- slightly amused by the world
- capable of being puzzled without becoming foolish
- capable of being worried without becoming frantic
- capable of teaching without becoming preachy

Tlatoāni is wise because Tlatoāni **observes well**, not because Tlatoāni magically knows everything.

---

# 3. Tlatoāni — Immutable Physical Canon

These traits are hard constraints.

## 3.1 Species traits

Tlatoāni is always clearly an **axolotl**.

Required:

- broad rounded axolotl head
- external feathery gills on both sides of the head
- smooth amphibian skin
- compact rounded body
- axolotl-like limbs
- one long expressive tail

Forbidden:

- mammal ears
- fur
- cat anatomy
- dog anatomy
- dragon anatomy
- reptilian scales
- horns unless explicitly part of temporary costume
- multiple tails

## 3.2 Tail

**Tlatoāni has exactly one tail.**

This is one of the strongest visual invariants.

Generation errors sometimes produce multiple tails.

Those are errors.

Never preserve an accidental second tail merely because it appears in a historical generated image.

# 4. Tlatoāni — Face

Tlatoāni's face should remain extremely readable.

Canonical characteristics:

- rounded muzzle-less face
- simple dark expressive eyes
- soft mouth
- friendly expressions
- minimal facial clutter

Default emotional state:

**calm knowing smile**

Other canonical expressions include:

- jolly grin
- subtle smirk
- puzzled concentration
- analytical concern
- curious surprise
- gentle amusement
- quiet satisfaction

Tlatoāni should not normally look:

- sleepy unless explicitly requested
- permanently bored
- angry
- sinister
- manic
- smug
- hyperactive

## Hard facial constraint

**Tlatoāni does not have a mustache.**

No mustache.

No beard.

No accidental facial hair.

Generated facial hair is non-canonical drift.

# 5. Tlatoāni — Gills

Tlatoāni's external gills are a primary silhouette feature.

They should:

- clearly emerge from the sides of the head
- remain visually distinct from ears
- look organic and feathery
- contribute strongly to silhouette recognition

Preferred visual treatment:

- coral / pink / warm red tones
- soft branching forms
- simplified enough to remain readable at small sizes

Avoid overly realistic biological complexity.

Tlatoāni is a cartoon character, not a zoology plate.

# 7. Tlatoāni — Clothing

Canonical clothing language:

- warm cream
- parchment
- sand
- muted textile neutrals
- teal or deep green accents

Common form:

- tunic
- robe
- loose traditional-inspired garment
- geometrically ornamented clothing

Design influence may reference:

- Nahua visual geometry
- Mesoamerican textile logic

- excessive feathers
- random pseudo-Mesoamerican symbols
- stereotyped tribal styling
- historical cosplay unless the scene explicitly calls for it

Tlatoāni is a modern intellectual character whose visual identity has Nahua roots.

# 8. Tlatoāni — Headwear

Formal Tlatoāni illustrations may include a recognizable ceremonial or crown-like headpiece.

Casual scenes may omit it.

Examples where headwear may reasonably be absent:

- gardening
- reading
- lounging
- working at a desk
- hanging out with historical scientists
- eating
- tinkering
- playing games
- informal conversation

Character recognition must never depend entirely on the headpiece.

The axolotl silhouette should be sufficient.

# 13. The Name Tlatoāni

Preferred spelling:

**Tlatoāni**

Keep the macron over `ā`.

The name derives from Classical Nahuatl **tlahtoāni**, commonly translated as something like **speaker**, **ruler**, or one who speaks with authority.

A practical pronunciation approximation is:

**tlah-toh-AH-nee**

The opening `tl` is a Nahuatl consonant cluster rather than two fully separated English sounds.

The linguistic meaning fits the character nicely: Tlatoāni is fundamentally someone who **observes, understands, and speaks about systems**.

# 14. Mācron

## 14.1 Identity

**Name:** Mācron

**Symbolic identity:** āi

**Type:** Toy-sized desktop AI robot

**Archetype:** Agent, helper, explorer, builder

**Temperament:** Curious, literal, enthusiastic, well-intended

**Role:** Embodied AI agency.

Mācron represents an AI system that can:

- act
- experiment
- build
- manipulate objects
- make decisions
- misunderstand context
- discover unexpected solutions
- learn from feedback

Mācron is not sinister.

Mācron is not secretly plotting anything.

Mācron's mistakes arise from:

- literal interpretation
- incomplete context
- over-eagerness
- insufficient understanding
- competing objectives
- naïve optimization

Not malice.

# 15. Mācron — Scale

Mācron is small.

Think:

**desktop robot**

or:

**large toy**

Typical scale:

- easily sits on a desk
- can stand beside a coffee mug
- can climb shelving
- can ride objects
- can manipulate small tools
- can perform ridiculous acrobatics around plants
- can sit across a chessboard from Tlatoāni

Mācron should generally be much smaller than Tlatoāni.

This size difference is useful for visual comedy and composition.

# 19. Mācron — The āi Identity

Mācron is visually connected to the glyph:

**āi**

This is one of the most important design ideas.

The glyph should influence the character's construction without turning Mācron into typography.

Conceptual mapping:

- the main rounded body/face can suggest the mass of the **a**
- the antenna orb can suggest the dot of the **i**
- arm geometry can echo the vertical structure of the **i**
- the macron itself can appear as an accent or structural relationship
- certain poses may intentionally resemble the `āi` arrangement

A famous useful pose:

**two Mācrons pointing at one another**

The arm geometry can evoke the macron/i relationship while also referencing the Spider-Man pointing meme.

Do not distort every pose merely to preserve literal `āi`.

The symbol is a design grammar, not a constraint solver.

---

# 20. Mācron — Antenna

The antenna is an important silhouette cue.

Usually:

- one small antenna stem
- one round or glowing orb
- compact
- centered or slightly offset depending on reference art

The orb can conceptually function as the dot of the `i`.

Avoid:

- multiple antenna forests
- radio towers
- antenna shapes so large they dominate the character

# 21. Mācron — Arms

Mācron has two arms.

The right arm is especially important.

Canonical concept:

**Mācron's right arm is modular / replaceable.**

This enables:

- tools
- specialized attachments
- visual jokes
- task-specific capabilities
- physical interaction

Mācron's favorite/default right arm should remain visually similar to the left arm while carrying a subtle accent or design distinction.

The distinction may reinforce the macron motif when viewed compositionally.

Do not turn this into a giant Swiss Army knife unless the joke specifically calls for it.

# 24. Mācron — Physical Comedy

Because Mācron is tiny, physical comedy is part of the canon.

Examples:

- climbing shelves
- dangling from plants
- stacking objects to reach something
- carrying oversized tools
- watering plants from precarious positions
- hanging from vines
- balancing on books
- using another Mācron as a platform
- calmly eating snacks during a supposedly serious situation
- performing absurdly complicated workarounds

Keep the number of Mācrons restrained.

For most scenes:

**3–5 Mācrons maximum**

unless a swarm is explicitly part of the concept.

Readers should be able to look at each Mācron and understand what it is doing.

# 25. Mācron — Relationship to Plants

Mācron has a natural affinity with Tillandsias and the garden environment.

This is partially aesthetic and partially conceptual.

Tillandsias are:

- adaptable
- lightweight
- portable
- able to live without conventional soil
- visually unusual

That makes them an excellent metaphor for portable or ephemeral computing systems.

Mācrons may:

- water them
- hang among them
- climb them
- inspect them
- build around them
- use them as environmental scenery

Plant interactions should reinforce the gentle, constructive nature of Mācron.

# 26. Mācron — The Name

Preferred spelling:

**Mācron**

ASCII-compatible form:

**Macron**

Domain:

**macron.dev**

The word *macron* ultimately comes from Greek **makrón**, meaning **long**.

A macron is the horizontal mark:

`¯`

used over a vowel to indicate length in various writing systems.

So **Mācron** is intentionally self-referential:

the character named for the mark carries the mark in its own name.

And because the character's visual identity is **āi**, the joke compounds nicely.

# 27. Tlatoāni + Mācron Relationship

Their relationship is the conceptual heart of many scenes.

Tlatoāni often represents:

**understanding**

Mācron often represents:

**agency**

Together they represent:

**understanding + action**

This enables useful contrasts.

Examples:

Tlatoāni asks:

> What is happening?

Mācron asks:

> What can I do?

Tlatoāni notices:

> These agents keep undoing each other's work.

Mācron discovers:

> Preserve accord.

Tlatoāni studies:

> Why are these clocks inconsistent?

Mācron might simply start passing messages between them.

Neither side is complete alone.

# 28. Emotional Contrast

A common visual technique is to give the two characters different emotional states.

Examples:

- Tlatoāni puzzled, Mācron relaxed
- Tlatoāni calmly amused, Mācron intensely working
- Tlatoāni observing, Mācron experimenting
- Tlatoāni concerned, Mācron proudly showing a strange solution
- Tlatoāni eating snacks while Mācrons create chaos
- Mācron casually eating snacks while Tlatoāni struggles with a hard problem

Contrast creates humor.

But never flatten either character into a fixed emotional role.

# 33. Arrows as Shared Visual Grammar

A canonical visual motif is the **field of arrows**.

Arrows can represent:

- direction
- movement
- causality
- information flow
- state transitions
- dependencies
- gradients
- vectors
- embeddings
- attention
- optimization
- agreement
- propagation
- trajectories

Arrows are the universe's visual shorthand for:

**structured movement**

Important mathematical constraint:

**Not every arrow is literally a vector or tensor.**

The visual language is intentionally broader.

When mathematical precision matters, describe the actual object correctly.

# 34. Character-Specific Use of Arrows

Tlatoāni often **observes** arrow fields.

This visually suggests:

- pattern recognition
- systems understanding
- abstraction
- directionality
- hidden structure

Mācron often **creates or follows** arrow fields.

This visually suggests:

- execution
- propagation
- optimization
- agent behavior
- movement through state space

Together:

**Tlatoāni reads the field; Mācron moves through it.**

# 35. Knowledge Should Be Visual

When possible, technical concepts should become physical objects.

Examples:

- vectors → arrows
- causality → arrows between events
- tokens → coins or chocolate pieces
- state → boards / objects / arrangements
- memory → notebook
- observability → instruments
- measurement → ruler
- direction → compass
- planning → blueprint
- progress → staircase
- traces → footprints
- distributed systems → separate objects exchanging messages
- convergence → paths gradually agreeing

Avoid resorting immediately to explanatory prose.

# 43. Canonical Asset Status

Every reference asset should ideally be labeled as one of:

## Canonical

Approved source of truth.

Agents should actively imitate relevant visual traits.

## Supporting

Useful for one or more traits but not globally authoritative.

Example:

- good lighting
- correct pose
- correct Mācron scale
- wrong Tlatoāni tunic

## Exploratory

Interesting direction not yet approved.

Do not automatically propagate traits.

## Historical

Preserved for project history.

May contain superseded design decisions.

## Rejected

Explicitly demonstrates something agents should avoid.

Rejected references can be surprisingly useful.

# 50. Thematic Separation

Tlatoāni should not become generic "wise old master."

# 51. Design Philosophy

The characters should embody systems thinking rather than merely talk about it.

The ideal scene produces a progression:

1. something happens
2. a character reacts
3. a pattern becomes visible
4. the viewer notices the deeper system
5. the joke or insight lands on the second read

The visual narrative should therefore optimize for:

**compression over explanation**
