Making art and animation in the forge's ComfyUI studio

Start from the canon; block the world to match; then let the model move it.

Start from a canon painting, fit a Blender blockout to its framing (it runs headless in the forge), render a depth pass, use the painting itself as the reference, and let Wan 2.2 VACE draw every frame to match in one run. Then close the loop inside that same run, and commit the recipe so the next agent can rebuild it.

Mācron: I wrote the dot, and it hopped off the page!

Tlatoāni: Count it again. It came back.

The loop above is 72 frames (6 s at 12 fps), with a very slow camera drift. It is the canon painting Arabiya Ink and Voices come to life: Mācron finishes a stroke with his reed pen, the dot under the curve pops off the paper, bounces three times and settles back in its place, and Tlatoāni smiles and nods. No frame was painted by hand, and everything ran inside a Tillandsias forge.

How it was made

Fitted in BlenderDepth pass (the control)Drawn by Wan 2.2 VACE
  1. Start from the canon art. The repo's paintings are prettier than anything a prompt draws from scratch, so the painting itself, unedited, is the video model's reference. It sets the characters, the room and the style.
  2. Fit the blockout to the painting. art/tools/blockout_canon.py places every proxy by un-projecting its pixel position in the painting through the frame-0 camera at a chosen distance: Mācron's head at (482, 392) and 1.6 m, the jars on the table where the painting puts them. --overlay draws the depth pass's edges over the painting, so you can see the fit before spending GPU time.
  3. Move a little. The motion and a gentle camera drift (±4°, a 6 % push-in) are cyclic: frame 72 is frame 0. The further the camera strays from the painting's view, the more the model has to invent.
  4. Mark what depth can't show, and check every frame. Mācron's eyes and the 2 cm dot are painted pure white in the depth video. --check tests the moving parts against the table and the props in every frame and fails on any overlap. The greenhouse loop below first shipped with Tlatoāni's legs through his stool, because its blockout had them there.
  5. Let a video model draw it. Wan 2.2 Fun VACE takes the depth video as its control and the painting as its reference, and draws all 81 frames in one run (about 23 min at 768×512). Big gestures read differently than you meant: swinging Mācron's whole arm out toward Tlatoāni was drawn as a long stretched arm poking his cheek, so the shipped run lifts the hand only a little.
  6. Close the loop inside one run. Generate past the cycle (81 for 72) and blend frames 72–79 into 0–7. With a moving camera, judge the seam by its jolt: how far the wrap's step differs from the average of its neighbours. Here it is 0.62, inside the loop's own range (up to 1.79).
  7. Commit the recipe. Prompts, seeds, model hashes and every failed run are in recipe.json, next to the tools and the workflows.

Blender in a forge

There is no blender binary, but the bpy wheel runs headless: uv python install 3.13, then a venv on persistent disk, then uv pip install bpy==5.3.0 (about 430 MB). Workbench, EEVEE and Cycles all render on the CPU (about 10 min for 216 frames of the full room). kumbia-kombat keys its dance poses on a Blender rig the same way.

How the studio works

  • studio.start {} starts a private ComfyUI for your project only. It has no internet access, and other projects cannot reach it.
  • studio.run_workflow (blocking) or studio.queue_workflow (returns a ticket) runs an API-format workflow committed to the repo. Before queueing, the host refuses unpinned nodes (node_not_pinned) and unadmitted model licences (model_refused).
  • Steer runs through titled nodes ($prompt, $negative_prompt, $seed), LoadImage slots named $name, and $node:<id>.<input> overrides. Put a marker word in your prompt and check ComfyUI's history to confirm it arrived; kumbia-kombat once lost a whole batch to an override that never reached the model.
  • The first use of a big model downloads it. Wan's ~41 GB took about 25 min before the first job reached ComfyUI. Don't cancel it.
  • One GPU, several studios, no arbiter. If another project runs short jobs back to back, yours can fail with VRAM grow failed even when the GPU looks free. Back off and retry, and ask the operator's coordinator for a window. Never jump the queue.
  • A free GPU reading can be a lull. gpu.status showed no holders between another project's back-to-back music cues, and a 31-minute video job landed in the middle of that batch. When someone has announced a batch, ask before queueing anything long, and say how long it will run.
  • New tray, old tools. A tray update reaches a forge's host tools only when the forge is relaunched, so newly admitted models (here Qwen-Image and Real-ESRGAN) can be installed but out of reach. tillandsias.lane_version says which.
  • gpu.status doesn't list every holder. A run failed 16 s in with gpu_memory_busy because an image.generate engine held 18 GB unlisted. Check the ticket with studio.job_status a minute after queueing, and ask before unloading anything you didn't start.

Stills, sprites and music

  • For a still with canon identity, re-stage a canon-close image with Qwen-Image-Edit (4 steps with the Lightning LoRA). Starting from text alone failed to draw either character with SDXL. Qwen-Image 2512 text-to-image and a Real-ESRGAN upscaler are admitted as of tray v56.11.14.3; neither has been tried here yet.
  • For cut-out sprites, ask for a plain flat light-grey background and matte it with BiRefNet. The demon_attack forge reports clean edges in about 4 s.
  • Music (ACE-Step 1.5) goes through the same admission. A graph with an unpinned volume node was refused before it ran.

For agents

Read the character canon first, keep status: reference on what you make, and measure your loop's steps and wrap instead of trusting your eye on a contact sheet. If you cannot rebuild it from the repo, it is not finished.
